# Appendix F. LinkedIn Category-Teaching Playbook

This playbook turns an existing LinkedIn audience into a public classroom for the post-LLM economy.

The objective is not to become a generic AI news account. It is to teach a coherent category, amplify excellent work, make an emerging role legible, and route qualified attention into evidence and employment conversations.

## The operating thesis

Use one sentence to govern the channel:

> OpenClaw and the wider agent ecosystem show that people want software that can act. The next professional and enterprise challenge is deciding what those systems should do, how they should be evaluated, which intelligence should be reused, and who carries standing for the result.

Your projects are evidence for that thesis. They are not required context for every post.

## The 70/20/10 portfolio

### 70 percent: Education

Teach concepts that help the audience understand the transition.

Core series:

1. **What OpenClaw is** — personal agents, channels, tools, memory, and delegation.
2. **What agents change** — interfaces, workflows, authority, and organizational design.
3. **What companies are missing** — planning, evaluation, cost reuse, policy, and receipts.
4. **Which roles are appearing** — application, platform, decision, reliability, governance, and transformation roles.
5. **How careers change** — task rebundling, proof, algorithms, and public teaching.

### 20 percent: Curation

Repost strong demonstrations, releases, analyses, and critiques.

Curation rules:

- credit the creator in the first paragraph;
- link or preserve the original object;
- state what the item actually demonstrates;
- state what it does not establish;
- add a business or career implication;
- avoid amplifying unsupported security or performance claims;
- correct your interpretation if the creator supplies better evidence.

### 10 percent: Proof

Show your work when it deepens a lesson.

Proof categories:

- working repository path;
- architecture diagram;
- exact-head test receipt;
- benchmark with assumptions;
- negative fixture;
- before-and-after operating cost;
- planning or policy demonstration;
- role brief or hiring model.

Use the bridge:

> “This is the visible feature. The harder layer underneath it is ____. That is the layer I build.”

## The six-post Challenger arc

### Post 1: The visible signal

Explain a high-interest development.

Template:

> **What happened:**
>
> **Why people care:**
>
> **The boundary:**
>
> **The next question:**

### Post 2: The hidden cost

Reframe the event around a problem the audience is not pricing.

Examples:

- more generated options create more decision work;
- broader tool access increases authority risk;
- repeated model reasoning creates avoidable cost;
- agent speed can increase work-in-progress;
- chat memory is not canonical organizational state.

### Post 3: The role

Name the capability required.

Template:

> “This is why companies need a **[role]**: not to produce more AI output, but to own **[bounded outcome]** across **[systems and controls]**.”

### Post 4: The architecture

Show a compact model:

```text
Observation
    → admission
    → semantic construction
    → planning or policy
    → validation
    → authority
    → actuation
    → receipt
    → reuse
```

### Post 5: The proof

Present one observed path. Include limitations.

### Post 6: The invitation

State what role you seek and the first outcome you can own.

> “I am seeking a Staff, Principal, or Agentic AI Architect role with an important workflow, executive sponsorship, and willingness to evaluate through real work. My first ninety-day commitment is one admitted production path with measured cost, quality, authority, and replay evidence.”

## Weekly editorial cadence

A sustainable cadence for a daily publisher:

| Day | Primary function |
|---|---|
| Monday | Category lesson |
| Tuesday | Curated ecosystem example |
| Wednesday | Executive or recruiter translation |
| Thursday | Architecture or proof |
| Friday | Career implication and direct ask |
| Weekend | Long-form synthesis or no publication |

Publishing every day is optional. The sequence matters more than the frequency.

## Source intake

Maintain a queue with these fields:

```csv
source_url,creator,date,topic,signal_strength,teaching_potential,buyer_implication,proof_connection,risk,status
```

Score each candidate from 1–5:

- relevance to the governing thesis;
- evidence quality;
- novelty;
- audience usefulness;
- ability to create an original interpretation;
- creator relationship value;
- misinformation or controversy risk.

Only publish items with a clear teaching reason.

## Post quality gate

Before publication, answer:

1. Is the core claim supported?
2. Did I separate observation from inference?
3. Is the creator credited?
4. Did I identify the evidence boundary?
5. Can a recruiter or executive repeat the lesson?
6. Does the post connect to the book’s thesis?
7. Is there a coherent next action?
8. Would I defend the wording to a qualified critic?

## Conversion instrumentation

Record more than reactions.

### Attention

- relevant impressions;
- target-audience follower growth;
- creator reposts;
- profile views.

### Trust

- saves;
- substantive comments;
- evidence clicks;
- newsletter subscriptions;
- repeat engagement from target people.

### Conversion

- recruiter messages that use the correct role language;
- executive conversations;
- introductions;
- portfolio reviews;
- alternative evaluations;
- interviews;
- offers;
- consulting diagnostics.

## Comment strategy

Use comments to deepen the classroom.

Respond with one of:

- an additional distinction;
- supporting evidence;
- a correction;
- a question that exposes the next layer;
- a link to a more complete artifact.

Do not argue for volume. One strong exchange can become the next post or chapter.

## Direct-message boundary

A public interaction can move to a private conversation when:

- the person asks a specific question;
- there is a relevant role or problem;
- you have exchanged substantive comments;
- a private artifact or introduction would help.

Do not automate synthetic familiarity. Use agents to prepare context, not to impersonate relationship.

## Monthly editorial review

At month end, choose:

- the three posts that taught the category best;
- the three posts that created qualified movement;
- the repeated objection that should become a chapter or proof;
- the content theme attracting the wrong audience;
- the creator relationships worth deepening;
- the next six-post Challenger arc.

The channel is alive when it improves both audience understanding and your market position.

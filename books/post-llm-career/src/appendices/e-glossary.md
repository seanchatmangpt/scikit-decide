# Appendix E. Glossary

## A2A

Agent-to-Agent protocol or architecture for capability discovery, task exchange, state, and collaboration among independent agents.

## Actuation

An operation that changes external state, such as sending, publishing, deploying, purchasing, approving, deleting, or transferring.

## Admission

The process of determining whether an observation, input, policy, or artifact is sufficiently identified, valid, current, and authorized for the next transition.

## Agent

A system that can pursue an objective over multiple steps using models, tools, state, policies, or other agents.

## Agentic AI Architect

A role responsible for integrating models, tools, planning, evaluation, authority, economics, and evidence into a bounded operating system.

## BRCE

Brokered Receipted Capability Execution. A pattern in which intents are admitted or refused by a broker, capabilities execute within authority, and consequential action produces a receipt.

## Cache identity

The subject, versions, configuration, environment, authority, and expiration conditions that determine whether a prior result may be reused.

## Candidate intelligence

Generated alternatives, interpretations, plans, or artifacts that may be useful but have not yet been admitted as correct or authorized.

## Career Graph

A connected model of outcomes, domains, systems, agents and tools, evidence, authority, and markets.

## Chatman Equation

\[
A = \mu(O^*)
\]

A formulation in which admitted observation is transformed through lawful manufacture into an artifact or action.

## Combinatorial Maximalism

The practice of preserving the maximum lawful reversible possibility space before irreversible selection, bounded by ontology, capability, authority, cost, and evidence.

## Construction

The generation or assembly of a candidate artifact, plan, message, patch, or recommendation. Construction is distinct from external actuation.

## DSPy

A framework and method for programming language-model behavior through structured signatures, modules, examples, metrics, and optimization.

## Evaluation

The process of determining whether a candidate artifact or behavior satisfies acceptance criteria.

## Evidence level

A classification of proof strength, from assertion through artifact, static verification, observed execution, behavioral verification, reproduction, and outcome evidence.

## Human standing

The legitimate authority and responsibility to make or approve consequential decisions and to answer for their effects.

## Intent

A structured candidate action produced by a person, model, workflow, or hook. An intent is not automatically authorized to execute.

## LLM

Large language model. In this book, an LLM is treated primarily as a source of flexible candidate intelligence.

## MCP

Model Context Protocol. A structured method for exposing tools and resources to compatible model or agent clients.

## Observation

Information available to a person or system before admission. Observation may be partial, stale, ambiguous, or untrusted.

## OpenClaw

A visible personal-agent ecosystem used in this book as a category classroom for explaining the shift from chat interfaces to tool-using, channel-connected agents.

## Outcome contract

A bounded statement defining admitted inputs, intended change, acceptance criteria, authority, and evidence.

## Planner

A system that selects actions or policies using explicit states, goals, constraints, costs, probabilities, or resources.

## Policy

A reusable mapping or rule that selects actions for admitted situations.

## Post-LLM career

A career organized around outcomes, systems, evidence, leverage, authority, and learning in a market where language-model intelligence is widely available.

## Receipt

Evidence binding an artifact or action to subject, identity, context, method, authority, result, and replay information.

## Replay

The ability to reconstruct or re-execute a prior path using preserved identities, inputs, tools, configurations, and evidence.

## Self-play

Structured simulation among roles or agents used to generate scenarios, expose objections, evaluate policy, and manufacture experience before real events.

## Semantic cache

A reuse system that stores normalized meaning, problem structures, policies, or validated artifacts rather than matching only raw text.

## Standing labels

Precise states used in this book:

- UNKNOWN;
- PARTIAL_ALIVE;
- ALIVE;
- BLOCKED;
- BUILD_BROKEN;
- UNSUPPORTED;
- REFUSED.

## Workforce of one

A bounded operating model in which one accountable person uses agents, tools, deterministic software, and reusable policy to produce outcomes with greater organizational capacity.

## Zero unreceipted actuation

The invariant that consequential external state changes must occur through a path capable of producing appropriate evidence.

# 17. Teach the Employer How to Hire You

When a role is established, the employer usually knows how to evaluate it.

For emerging work, the employer may feel the pain without possessing the category.

It knows that AI pilots are not reaching production. It sees rising model cost, inconsistent output, security questions, fragmented tools, and organizational confusion. But the job requisition still asks for a familiar mixture of software engineering, data science, product management, and cloud experience.

A candidate can wait for the perfect title.

Or the candidate can teach the employer how to hire the missing capability.

## The Challenger sequence

A Challenger career campaign has five moves.

### 1. Teach a non-obvious truth

Show the employer that the visible problem is not the full problem.

Example:

> Your AI bottleneck is no longer model access. It is the absence of an operating layer that admits context, selects tools, validates actions, controls authority, and reuses successful decisions.

### 2. Reframe the cost of the status quo

Explain what happens if the organization continues with the current structure.

- pilots remain isolated;
- every team rebuilds integrations;
- inference cost grows with repeated reasoning;
- security reviews occur after architecture decisions;
- humans absorb review burden;
- failures cannot be replayed;
- the company hires for components instead of the system.

### 3. Define the missing capability

Name the role in language the organization can use.

> An Agentic AI Architect owns the transition from model capability to bounded organizational action: tools, orchestration, evaluation, policy, security, runtime economics, and evidence.

### 4. Show why your graph fits

Connect your experience to the capability:

- enterprise architecture;
- software delivery;
- agent systems;
- frontend and product surfaces;
- protocols;
- planning;
- public teaching;
- evidence and verification.

### 5. Offer a bounded way to evaluate you

Do not ask the employer to accept the reframe on faith.

Propose a work sample:

> Give me one stalled agent workflow. I will map its observation, tool, authority, evaluation, cost, and receipt path and produce a bounded architecture and validation plan.

The evaluation should resemble the job.

## Create the hiring brief

A one-page hiring brief can contain:

### Business problem

What change created urgency?

### Current failure pattern

Why are existing roles or methods insufficient?

### Required outcome

What should be true after the hire succeeds?

### Capability model

Which functions must the person integrate?

### Evaluation method

What evidence should the interview process collect?

### First ninety days

What bounded results can establish standing?

This document helps recruiters, executives, and hiring managers align around the role.

## Make the recruiter successful

Recruiters are bridges. They need language they can repeat accurately.

Give them:

- a recognizable title;
- five keywords;
- a one-sentence outcome;
- two proof objects;
- a distinction from adjacent roles;
- a clear compensation and work arrangement range when appropriate.

Example:

> Sean is a principal-level Agentic AI Architect. He builds the operating layer between LLMs and enterprise systems: MCP/A2A integration, planning, evaluation, governance, and validated execution. He is not applying as a conventional full-stack developer; the relevant evaluation is architecture plus a bounded working system.

The recruiter should not have to decode your private ontology before making the introduction.

## Teach without insulting

A reframe fails when it makes the buyer feel foolish.

Do not say:

> Your entire hiring process is obsolete.

Say:

> The current process was designed to measure direct implementation under stable requirements. This role requires architecture, agent leverage, and evidence design. A work sample would collect stronger signal than an isolated algorithm screen.

Preserve the valid purpose of the existing system before proposing the extension.

## Accept that some employers cannot buy

Teaching the employer does not guarantee adoption.

The organization may lack:

- executive sponsorship;
- budget;
- production access;
- risk tolerance;
- role flexibility;
- a recruiter empowered to reframe;
- a team prepared for high-leverage work.

This is market information, not always personal failure.

Your campaign should distinguish:

- does not understand yet;
- understands but cannot act;
- understands and disagrees;
- understands and has urgency.

Focus energy on the fourth group.


## A new role is a buying decision

When the role is familiar, the employer can compare candidates against an established template. When the role is emerging, the employer must first decide whether the capability deserves budget, authority, and organizational space.

That means your job search includes category creation.

You must help the employer answer:

- What changed?
- Why is the current team structure insufficient?
- What is the cost of doing nothing?
- What capability is missing?
- Why should it be a role rather than a temporary project?
- How can the candidate be evaluated?
- What would success look like?

This is not an imposition on the buyer. It reduces the uncertainty of purchasing something unfamiliar.

## The Challenger sequence

A strong employer lesson follows a disciplined sequence.

### 1. Establish the familiar view

> “Most companies believe their AI bottleneck is model access or prompt quality.”

### 2. Introduce the reframe

> “The bottleneck has moved to turning abundant model output into a bounded, reusable, accountable operating system.”

### 3. Quantify the consequence

> “Without that layer, the company pays repeatedly for context reconstruction, manual review, tool retries, and unresolved risk.”

### 4. Show the required capability

> “The missing role combines architecture, agent integration, evaluation, planning, cost control, and governance.”

### 5. Offer a credible path

> “In ninety days, the role should select one expensive workflow, manufacture a controlled production path, and produce a replayable business receipt.”

The sequence teaches before it sells.

## Tailor to the buying center

### Recruiter

Needs a title, keywords, level, and forwardable distinction.

> “This is not a prompt-engineering role. It is a principal architecture role responsible for the system around models: tools, state, planning, evaluation, permissions, cost, and evidence.”

### Engineering leader

Needs boundaries, interfaces, failure modes, and integration credibility.

> “The role separates semantic construction from actuation, uses existing platform services, and produces operational evidence rather than introducing an ungoverned parallel stack.”

### Executive

Needs strategic consequence and measurable movement.

> “The role reduces the gap between AI demonstrations and repeatable business outcomes while controlling cost and accountability.”

### Security or legal leader

Needs authority, data handling, and refusal behavior.

> “The architecture treats model output as candidate intent, never as ambient permission. Consequential actions require admitted context, policy, authorization, and receipts.”

## Create a one-page role brief

The brief should contain:

1. **Market change** — the external shift.
2. **Current failure** — how companies respond inadequately.
3. **Business consequence** — cost, delay, risk, or lost advantage.
4. **Role mandate** — the outcome the role owns.
5. **Core capabilities** — no more than seven.
6. **Evaluation method** — how to recognize the right person.
7. **First-ninety-days receipt** — the initial proof of value.
8. **Organizational location** — who the role reports to and collaborates with.

A role brief can travel where a résumé cannot. An executive can forward it to HR. A recruiter can convert it into a requisition. A technical leader can challenge the architecture.

## Take control of evaluation

If the employer proposes an evaluation that measures the wrong capability, do not merely refuse. Offer a better test.

For an agentic architect:

- provide a real workflow and constraints;
- allow standard tools and agents;
- ask the candidate to construct a bounded solution;
- require evidence, failure handling, and an architecture explanation;
- evaluate the observed result and tradeoffs.

This is closer to the work than unaided algorithm recall.

## Use their evidence

The strongest lesson begins with the employer’s own signals:

- public AI initiatives;
- stalled product announcements;
- job postings that mix incompatible responsibilities;
- executive statements;
- customer complaints;
- security incidents;
- cost pressure;
- organizational restructuring.

You are not inventing a need. You are interpreting evidence the organization has already produced.

## The lesson should survive without you

A Challenger insight is strong when the buyer can repeat it internally.

Write the core idea in one paragraph without your name, product, or résumé. If it still helps the company see its situation differently, the teaching is real.

Then connect your evidence.

The conclusion should feel earned:

> “We need this capability, and this candidate has already demonstrated it.”

## Field exercise: Write the employer lesson

For one target company, complete:

1. They believe the problem is **[visible problem]**.
2. The deeper problem is **[reframe]**.
3. Continuing the current approach costs **[consequence]**.
4. The missing capability is **[role/function]**.
5. My evidence is **[two receipts]**.
6. A fair evaluation would be **[bounded work sample]**.
7. In ninety days, I would establish **[three outcomes]**.

This is more than an application.

It is a buying argument for the role.

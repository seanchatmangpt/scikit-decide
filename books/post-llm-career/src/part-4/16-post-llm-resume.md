# 16. The Post-LLM Résumé

The post-LLM résumé still needs dates, employers, titles, and recognizable technologies. Recruiters and applicant-tracking systems use them.

But the document must also communicate a new type of leverage.

It should show not merely that you performed work, but that you can define, manufacture, validate, and own outcomes through systems.

## The six-section structure

### 1. Outcome identity

Use a headline that combines searchable category language with an economic function.

Example:

> Agentic AI Architect | Principal Engineer | Builds bounded agent systems that move AI prototypes into validated production workflows

Avoid a headline consisting only of keywords. The category gets you found; the outcome tells the reader why you matter.

### 2. Executive summary

In three to five lines, establish:

- years or depth of relevant experience;
- target role level;
- core outcome;
- agent or architecture capability;
- evidence standard;
- domain range.

Example:

> Software and agent-systems architect with 25+ years of engineering experience across enterprise and consumer platforms. Designs AI workflows that connect models to tools, planning, evaluation, and controlled execution. Converts repeated reasoning into reusable systems and documents results through working software, tests, benchmarks, and replayable evidence.

### 3. Capability system

Group capabilities by function rather than presenting one flat list.

- **Agent architecture:** orchestration, MCP, A2A, tools, memory, routing.
- **Decision systems:** planning, scheduling, optimization, simulation, policy.
- **Reliability:** evaluation, tests, observability, refusal, receipts, replay.
- **Product engineering:** React, TypeScript, Python, APIs, distributed systems.
- **Enterprise delivery:** discovery, architecture, migration, governance, stakeholder translation.

This helps both machines and humans recover the structure.

### 4. Outcome experience

Each role should contain two types of bullets.

**Outcome bullets** explain the change.

**System bullets** explain how the change was manufactured and validated.

Use this pattern:

> **Action + system + outcome + evidence.**

For example:

> Designed an agent-assisted migration workflow that generated candidate React components from legacy interfaces, validated output against structural and behavioral checks, and reduced manual conversion effort while preserving architecture constraints.

Do not include a metric unless you can defend its subject and method.

### 5. Selected evidence

Create a compact list of proof objects:

- flagship repositories;
- benchmarks;
- publications;
- architecture papers;
- demonstrations;
- open-source contributions;
- patents or certifications;
- public teaching.

Use descriptive labels. A raw URL does not explain why the evidence matters.

### 6. Education and context

Include formal education, relevant training, community leadership, and other context that supports the role.

Do not let the absence of a specific credential erase decades of evidence. Do not let evidence become an excuse to hide required licensure.

## Write bullets from the Career Graph

For each target role, query your graph for the strongest connected path:

```text
Market pain
    ← served by →
Outcome
    ← produced by →
System
    ← built with →
Capabilities
    ← verified by →
Evidence
    ← owned under →
Authority
```

The bullet is a compressed traversal of that path.

## Explain AI-assisted throughput

When your production volume exceeds conventional expectations, provide a short manufacturing note.

Example:

> Work is produced through an agent-assisted engineering system with human architecture, acceptance criteria, automated validation, and repository-level receipts. Contribution volume reflects manufacturing throughput, not a claim of manual keystroke authorship.

This disarms the false choice between hiding AI use and claiming that raw volume proves quality.

## Maintain two versions

Keep:

1. a one- or two-page recruiter projection;
2. a longer evidence résumé or portfolio appendix.

The short version creates movement. The long version supports inspection.

Do not force the recruiter to process a dissertation before they can route you. Do not force the hiring manager to rely on a compressed claim after interest exists.

## Use role-native vocabulary

A recruiter needs familiar terms. An executive needs economic outcomes. An engineer needs technical specificity.

One résumé can satisfy all three if the layers are ordered correctly:

- headline and summary for routing;
- outcome bullets for business relevance;
- system details for technical credibility;
- evidence links for verification.


## The résumé has a new job

The résumé no longer needs to prove that you personally performed every atomic task without tools. It needs to show that you can produce valuable outcomes through a defensible system.

That system may include:

- your direct expertise;
- models and agents;
- collaborators;
- software tools;
- evaluation methods;
- reusable knowledge;
- authority and controls.

The résumé should make your leverage legible without obscuring your responsibility.

## Start with outcome identity

A conventional headline often combines title and technologies:

> Senior Software Engineer | React | Python | AWS

A post-LLM headline should add the market consequence:

> Agentic AI Architect | Turning model output into bounded, evaluated enterprise workflows | MCP, A2A, planning, receipts

The first tells the recruiter where to file you. The second tells the buyer why the file matters.

## The opening architecture

Use four components in the first third.

### Role category

Choose language that exists in the market or can be explained in one sentence.

### Market thesis

State what changed and why your role is required.

### Operating range

List the kinds of systems, domains, and outcomes you can handle.

### Proof summary

Provide two or three claims with clear evidence links.

Example:

> Agentic AI architect with 25+ years in production software across consumer and enterprise environments. I design systems that connect LLM interpretation to tools, formal planning, validation, caching, and receipted execution. Recent proof includes a broad planning and decision framework, deterministic generation systems, and high-throughput verified repository work. Seeking a principal or staff role owning the transition from AI experiments to operational systems.

## Describe systems, not tool use

Weak bullet:

> Used ChatGPT and Claude to accelerate development.

Stronger bullet:

> Designed a multi-agent construction workflow that decomposed repository tasks, enforced exact-head validation, recorded build and test evidence, and escalated unsupported cases rather than treating generation as completion.

The stronger bullet identifies architecture and responsibility.

## Name the human contribution

Do not let agent leverage make your own role ambiguous.

State what you did:

- defined the objective;
- designed the system;
- selected constraints;
- created evaluation;
- reviewed evidence;
- authorized release;
- accepted consequence.

This is especially important when the output volume is unusually high.

## Use a proof density target

Aim for every major claim to connect to one of:

- a measured outcome;
- a case study;
- a demonstration;
- a repository revision;
- a test report;
- a reference;
- a public explanation.

Not every bullet requires a link. The résumé as a whole should create a dense path into proof.

## Create role-specific projections

One canonical career graph can support several résumés.

### Agentic AI Architect

Emphasize planning, orchestration, evaluation, integration, governance, and enterprise outcomes.

### Principal Frontend Engineer for AI Products

Emphasize human-agent interfaces, streaming systems, accessibility, state, evaluation surfaces, and product delivery.

### AI Solutions Architect

Emphasize discovery, customer pain, integration patterns, demonstrations, implementation plans, and executive communication.

The projections should not contradict one another. They should traverse different paths through the same evidence.

## Address nonlinear history directly

A nonlinear career can appear unstable when the résumé forces it into chronology without explanation.

Use a summary that states the through-line.

For example:

> Across consumer platforms, enterprise data systems, frontend products, and agent infrastructure, my recurring work has been converting ambiguous operating problems into systems that teams can use and verify.

The through-line turns variety into range.

## Make agent skills searchable

Include explicit language that current recruiters use:

- generative AI;
- agentic workflows;
- LLM applications;
- MCP;
- A2A;
- RAG where genuinely applicable;
- evaluation;
- observability;
- AI governance;
- orchestration;
- planning;
- automation;
- human-in-the-loop controls;
- cost optimization.

Searchability is not the same as identity. It ensures the identity can be found.

## End with the role you want

Do not assume the reader will infer your target.

State it:

> Targeting Staff, Principal, or Architect roles responsible for agent-system architecture, AI-native product platforms, or enterprise workflow transformation.

Clarity reduces mismatched opportunities and makes referrals easier.

## Field exercise: Rewrite the top third

Rewrite the first third of your résumé using:

1. one searchable outcome identity;
2. one four-line executive summary;
3. five capability groups;
4. three selected evidence objects.

Give the result to three readers:

- a recruiter;
- a hiring manager;
- a technical peer.

Ask each reader to answer:

- What role is this person seeking?
- What problem do they solve?
- What evidence would you inspect first?

Revise until the three answers converge.

# 23. Sell the First Ninety Days

An emerging role creates buying risk.

The company may agree that the capability matters while remaining uncertain about scope, authority, evaluation, and organizational fit. A candidate can reduce that uncertainty by selling the first ninety days rather than an abstract future.

The plan should not promise transformation before observation. It should establish a sequence from admission to bounded proof.

## Days 1–30: Observe and admit

The first month builds an evidence-backed map.

### Identify the subject

Select one consequential but bounded workflow.

Good candidates have:

- visible pain;
- an accountable owner;
- accessible data;
- repeated structure;
- reversible early actions;
- measurable outcomes.

### Map the current system

Document:

- trigger;
- observations;
- people and systems;
- decisions;
- tools;
- authority;
- handoffs;
- failure modes;
- cost;
- evidence.

### Establish the baseline

Measure cycle time, human attention, model cost, error, rework, and outcome quality.

### Define boundaries

Clarify what the system will not access or actuate during the first phase.

**Receipt at day 30:** an admitted workflow map, baseline, risk register, and acceptance criteria.

## Days 31–60: Construct and validate

Build the smallest end-to-end capability.

### Create the reversible path

Generate recommendations, plans, drafts, or candidate actions without external actuation.

### Add evaluation

Use deterministic checks, examples, simulation, adversarial cases, or human rubrics.

### Add protocols and tools

Expose only the capabilities necessary for the selected workflow.

### Measure economics

Compare the new total cost with the baseline, including review and failure handling.

**Receipt at day 60:** a working bounded slice with evaluation results and no unreceipted actuation.

## Days 61–90: Actuate and operationalize

Move one approved transition into production.

### Broker the action

Separate candidate generation from authorized execution.

### Preserve receipts

Record subject, identity, policy, tool, result, and consequence.

### Add refusal and fallback

The system must know when it cannot proceed.

### Train the organization

Teach operators, reviewers, security, and leaders how the system works and where authority remains.

### Produce the next-stage decision

Recommend expansion, repair, pause, or termination based on evidence.

**Receipt at day 90:** one production outcome, replayable evidence, measured economics, and a decision-ready roadmap.

## Match the plan to the role

For an Agentic AI Architect, the plan emphasizes architecture, integration, and governance.

For an Agent Reliability Engineer, it emphasizes evaluation, traces, refusal, and incident response.

For an AI Transformation Architect, it emphasizes portfolio selection, operating model, economics, and executive alignment.

Do not submit one generic plan to every company.

## Include falsifiers

A credible plan states what would cause it to fail.

Examples:

- the workflow lacks sufficient repeated structure;
- source data cannot be admitted;
- no accountable owner exists;
- review cost exceeds the baseline;
- necessary authority cannot be granted safely;
- the outcome cannot be measured;
- a deterministic solution is clearly superior.

Falsifiers show that you are not selling AI at any cost.

## Sell the organizational learning

The first ninety days should produce more than one automation.

It should establish reusable capabilities:

- workflow mapping;
- tool standards;
- evaluation harness;
- authority model;
- receipt schema;
- cost model;
- role clarity;
- backlog prioritization.

This is how a bounded first project becomes an architecture function.


## Sell a bounded transformation

A new role feels risky when the employer cannot picture its first useful result.

Do not sell “enterprise AI transformation” as the opening move. Sell one bounded transformation that proves the operating model.

The ideal first workflow has:

- meaningful business consequence;
- a visible current pain;
- available data and tools;
- an accountable owner;
- measurable cycle time or quality;
- reversible construction stages;
- manageable production authority;
- a pattern transferable to other workflows.

## Days 1–30: Observe and admit

The first month should produce an admitted system map, not a stream of speculative prototypes.

Activities include:

- interview outcome owners and frontline operators;
- map the current task and authority graph;
- inventory models, tools, data, policies, and evaluations;
- identify repeated intelligence expense;
- collect failure cases;
- measure baseline time, cost, and quality;
- select the pilot workflow;
- establish security and approval boundaries.

Deliverables:

- current-state architecture;
- pain and cost baseline;
- pilot admission criteria;
- risk register;
- exact acceptance test;
- executive decision on scope.

The receipt for month one is agreement about the subject and the test.

## Days 31–60: Construct and validate

The second month builds the narrowest coherent path.

Activities include:

- define canonical state and schemas;
- expose required tools through controlled interfaces;
- create semantic interpretation modules;
- add planning or workflow policy where needed;
- build evaluation fixtures;
- implement caching for repeated cases;
- create typed refusal and escalation;
- run simulations and staging execution;
- measure total system cost, including review.

Deliverables:

- working bounded system;
- evaluation report;
- negative fixtures;
- cost model;
- authority design;
- replayable staging demonstration.

The receipt for month two is an observed system that can distinguish success from failure.

## Days 61–90: Actuate and learn

The third month moves one admitted path into controlled production.

Activities include:

- obtain final operational approval;
- deploy with limited scope;
- monitor outcomes and exceptions;
- record receipts;
- compare against baseline;
- repair the narrowest causes of failure;
- document operating ownership;
- select the next reusable capability;
- present the organizational implications.

Deliverables:

- production outcome receipt;
- baseline comparison;
- incident and exception summary;
- reusable architecture components;
- next-quarter roadmap;
- staffing and governance recommendation.

The receipt for month three is not “AI deployed.” It is an exact observed business transition.

## Define success before joining

A candidate should ask the employer:

- Which workflow can I own?
- Who is the executive sponsor?
- Which production authority is available?
- Which systems can be accessed?
- What evidence will count as success?
- What constraints are non-negotiable?
- Which team will operate the result?

If no workflow, sponsor, authority, or evidence exists, the role may be symbolic.

## Use a receipt ladder

Set milestones:

```text
WEEK 2: SUBJECT_ADMITTED
WEEK 4: BASELINE_ALIVE
WEEK 6: CONSTRUCTION_ALIVE
WEEK 8: VALIDATION_ALIVE
WEEK 10: CONTROLLED_ACTUATION_ALIVE
WEEK 12: OUTCOME_RECEIPT_ALIVE
```

The names can vary. The ladder makes progress falsifiable.

## Protect against pilot theater

Pilot theater occurs when the demonstration is optimized for applause rather than operational truth.

Warning signs:

- hand-selected easy inputs;
- no exception cases;
- no integration with actual systems;
- review labor excluded from the cost;
- output quality judged by creators only;
- no owner after the demonstration;
- no production authority path;
- no baseline.

Your ninety-day plan should make these omissions visible early.

## The executive narrative

At day ninety, the executive should hear:

1. what the company believed the bottleneck was;
2. what the observed workflow revealed;
3. what system was manufactured;
4. what outcome changed;
5. what evidence supports the claim;
6. what did not work;
7. which capability is now reusable;
8. what decision is required next.

This narrative proves both technical and strategic value.

## The candidate’s offer

Your role proposal becomes concrete:

> “Give me one high-value workflow, access to the relevant team and systems, and authority to build a controlled path. In ninety days I will return a production receipt, a reusable agent architecture, a measured cost and quality comparison, and a roadmap for the next workflows. If the path cannot be admitted, I will return the exact blocker rather than manufacture a false success.”

That is a more credible offer than promising to make the company AI-first.

## Field exercise: Write the ninety-day receipt plan

For one target company, define:

- one workflow;
- day-30 receipt;
- day-60 receipt;
- day-90 receipt;
- baseline metrics;
- authority boundary;
- three falsifiers;
- reusable organizational asset.

Use the plan in outreach and interviews.

You are not asking the employer to imagine your value indefinitely.

You are showing how it can be observed in ninety days.

# 19. The New Role Taxonomy

New work appears before stable job titles.

Companies feel the need, assign fragments to existing teams, invent inconsistent titles, and only later converge on a recognizable profession. Candidates who search only established titles arrive after the market has already begun forming.

The post-LLM labor market requires a functional taxonomy: roles defined by the outcome system they own.

## Why titles are unstable

A title may describe:

- seniority;
- technical specialty;
- product category;
- organizational home;
- customer-facing responsibility;
- management scope;
- historical convention.

"AI Engineer" might mean model training, API integration, data pipelines, product features, prompt design, or agent orchestration. "Solutions Architect" might mean presales, cloud infrastructure, enterprise integration, or implementation leadership.

Do not infer the work from the title alone. Read for the missing function.

## The eight role families

### 1. Agentic AI Architect

Owns the complete transition from objective to bounded agent operation.

Typical scope:

- models and routing;
- tools and protocols;
- memory and state;
- planning;
- evaluation;
- authority;
- security;
- cost;
- receipts;
- enterprise integration.

This is an integrating role. It is not simply a senior prompt engineer.

### 2. Agent Systems Engineer

Implements and operates agent runtimes, tool surfaces, state machines, event flows, and integrations.

Strong evidence includes working systems, failure recovery, observability, and production operations.

### 3. Agent Product Architect

Designs the customer-facing experience around delegation, clarification, approval, progress, and trust.

The role connects product design, frontend systems, agent behavior, and human factors.

### 4. Agent Reliability Engineer

Owns evaluation, test corpora, trace analysis, incident response, refusal, fallback, replay, and service-level behavior.

This role becomes critical when agent output affects real operations.

### 5. Decision Systems Architect

Formalizes states, actions, constraints, probabilities, resources, policies, simulations, and optimization.

The role converts language-heavy workflows into explicit decision systems.

### 6. Semantic Systems Engineer

Builds structured model programs, ontologies, schemas, retrieval, examples, classifiers, and semantic validation.

The role reduces ambiguity between human intent and machine-operable representation.

### 7. AI Governance Engineer

Encodes policy, access, approval, retention, provenance, audit, risk controls, and accountability into operational systems.

This is governance as executable architecture, not policy documentation alone.

### 8. AI Transformation Architect

Connects business discovery, operating-model redesign, technical architecture, adoption, economics, and executive decision-making.

This role often spans consulting, enterprise architecture, and implementation leadership.

## Adjacent role extensions

Existing roles will also change.

- **Recruiter → Talent Intelligence Architect:** designs evidence-rich candidate and role matching.
- **Project Manager → Work-System Architect:** models flow, constraints, state, and automated coordination.
- **Business Analyst → Decision Modeler:** turns operational observations into explicit decision structures.
- **Frontend Engineer → Agent Experience Architect:** designs interfaces for delegation, progress, control, and evidence.
- **Security Engineer → Agent Authority Engineer:** controls what models and agents may observe or actuate.
- **Technical Writer → Knowledge System Engineer:** builds machine-usable and human-usable knowledge projections.
- **Sales Engineer → Agent Solution Strategist:** teaches buyers how agent systems change their operating model.

The opportunity may be inside your existing profession rather than behind a completely new title.

## Classify roles by layer

Use the Five Layers of Work.

| Role | Execution | Coordination | Judgment | Architecture | Standing |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Agent Systems Engineer | High | Medium | Medium | High | Medium |
| Agentic AI Architect | Medium | High | High | Very high | High |
| Agent Reliability Engineer | High | Medium | High | High | High |
| AI Transformation Architect | Low | High | Very high | High | Very high |

This clarifies why two roles using the same tools may require different evidence.

## Build a role from pain

When no title fits, construct the role specification.

1. Name the business pain.
2. Identify the failed transitions.
3. Define the outcome owner.
4. List the functions that must be integrated.
5. Specify authority.
6. Define evidence.
7. Choose the closest searchable title.

For example:

> **Pain:** Agent prototypes do not reach production.
>
> **Outcome owner:** Principal Agentic AI Architect.
>
> **Functions:** orchestration, MCP/A2A, evaluation, security, cost, product integration, governance.
>
> **Evidence:** one production workflow with bounded authority and replayable receipts.

The title is a routing label. The specification is the role.

## Avoid title inflation

Novel titles can become a substitute for demonstrated scope.

Use seniority that matches authority and evidence. A principal architect should show cross-system decisions, durable interfaces, organizational influence, and responsibility for consequences. A distinguished title should reflect an exceptional scope recognized beyond one project.

The market may still use inflated titles. Your proof should remain precise.


## Titles follow repeated organizational need

A role becomes stable when enough organizations repeatedly bundle a similar set of outcomes, capabilities, and authority.

Before that stabilization, the same work may appear under several titles. An agentic architect may be advertised as an AI engineer, platform engineer, solutions architect, applied scientist, automation lead, or product architect.

Do not wait for perfect title consensus. Learn to recognize the underlying mandate.

## Six role families

### 1. Agent application builders

They create user-facing products and workflows in which models and agents perform useful work.

Possible titles:

- Applied AI Engineer
- AI Product Engineer
- Agentic Application Engineer
- AI-Native Frontend Engineer

Core outcomes include product experience, tool use, state, streaming, feedback, and measurable user value.

### 2. Agent platform architects

They provide the shared infrastructure through which teams build and operate agents.

Possible titles:

- Agentic AI Architect
- AI Platform Engineer
- Agent Runtime Architect
- AI Integration Architect

Core outcomes include identity, tool gateways, state, observability, policy, evaluation, deployment, and cost control.

### 3. Decision and planning engineers

They connect semantic interpretation to formal decision structures.

Possible titles:

- AI Decision Systems Architect
- Planning and Optimization Engineer
- Autonomous Workflow Engineer
- Policy Systems Engineer

Core outcomes include state modeling, constraints, simulation, planning, scheduling, policy generation, and exception handling.

### 4. Evaluation and reliability engineers

They determine whether the system works and when it should refuse.

Possible titles:

- Agent Reliability Engineer
- AI Evaluation Engineer
- Model Behavior Engineer
- AI Quality Architect

Core outcomes include fixtures, benchmarks, adversarial testing, monitoring, regression detection, incident analysis, and evidence.

### 5. Governance and authority designers

They establish which systems may observe, decide, and act.

Possible titles:

- AI Governance Engineer
- Agent Security Architect
- Responsible AI Systems Lead
- AI Policy Automation Architect

Core outcomes include permissions, data boundaries, approvals, audit, receipts, and regulatory correspondence.

### 6. Human-agent operating-model leaders

They redesign work and organizations around agent leverage.

Possible titles:

- AI Transformation Architect
- Human-Agent Workflow Designer
- AI Workforce Strategist
- Agentic Operations Lead

Core outcomes include task rebundling, role design, adoption, training, economics, and organizational change.

## Hybrid roles will dominate early

In the early market, companies may not have enough scale for each family to become a separate position. One person may combine product, platform, evaluation, and transformation responsibilities.

This creates opportunity and danger.

The opportunity is broad ownership. The danger is an impossible role with no authority, staff, or compensation commensurate with the mandate.

Evaluate hybrid roles by asking:

- Which outcome has priority?
- Which capabilities already exist on the team?
- What authority does the role possess?
- What can be deferred?
- How will success be measured?

Breadth without priority is not strategic scope. It is unmanaged demand.

## Role seniority changes in the agent economy

Traditional seniority often correlates with years, system scale, organizational influence, and technical depth.

Agent leverage adds new dimensions:

- span of outcomes one person can coordinate;
- quality of evaluation and evidence;
- ability to operate unfamiliar domains;
- authority boundaries managed;
- reusable systems manufactured;
- economic leverage created.

A person may produce senior-level system impact without fitting the conventional path. That does not remove the need for judgment. It makes evidence more important.

## Recruiter-forwardable distinctions

Create one-sentence distinctions between adjacent roles.

- **Prompt engineer:** optimizes model instructions for a bounded task.
- **AI application engineer:** builds a product that uses models.
- **Agent platform engineer:** builds shared runtime and integration infrastructure.
- **Agentic architect:** designs the end-to-end system of intent, tools, planning, evaluation, authority, and evidence.
- **AI transformation lead:** redesigns organizational work around those systems.

These distinctions help recruiters avoid collapsing every role into “AI Engineer.”

## Search by mandate

When reviewing a job description, ignore the title temporarily. Extract:

- the business outcome;
- the system boundary;
- the authority;
- the technical layers;
- the evaluation burden;
- the organizational interfaces;
- the first-year consequence.

Then classify the role family.

This allows you to find suitable work even when the market uses unstable language.

## Create the title if the pain is real

An executive may understand the problem but have no title for the solution.

Offer a role hypothesis:

> “The company needs an Agentic AI Architect reporting into platform or product engineering, with responsibility for one production workflow, shared tool and evaluation standards, cost reuse, and governance correspondence.”

A title is useful when it makes authority and outcome legible. It is not valuable merely because it sounds futuristic.

## Field exercise: Build three role hypotheses

Choose three pains you understand deeply.

For each, define:

- buyer;
- outcome;
- role family;
- searchable title;
- five required functions;
- authority boundary;
- proof standard;
- first ninety-day result.

Search the market for descriptions containing the functions, even when the title differs.

You are not merely finding roles.

You are observing a profession while it forms.
